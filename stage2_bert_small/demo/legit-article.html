Phishing

Phishing is a form of social engineering in which an attacker deceives a recipient into revealing credentials, payment details or other sensitive information. The term was coined in the mid-1990s by members of the America Online community, who described the practice of "fishing" for account passwords using instant messages that appeared to come from official support staff.

Techniques

The most common technique is a spoofed message that imitates a trusted organisation and directs the recipient to a website controlled by the attacker. The website reproduces the layout of the genuine service so that the recipient enters credentials without suspicion. More recent variants include spear phishing, which targets a specific individual using information gathered from public sources, and clone phishing, in which a genuine message is copied and its links replaced.

Detection and mitigation

Defences fall into three broad groups: user education, technical filtering and authentication. Filtering systems typically examine the message body, the sending domain and the structure of embedded links. Authentication standards such as SPF, DKIM and DMARC allow a receiving server to verify that a message genuinely originated from the domain it claims to represent. Multi-factor authentication limits the value of a stolen password, because possession of the password alone is no longer sufficient to gain access.

Research

Academic work in this area frequently studies the problem as a text classification task, comparing lexical features, character n-grams and learned representations produced by transformer models. Reported results depend heavily on the corpus used, and cross-corpus generalisation remains an open problem because the vocabulary of real campaigns changes quickly.
